# Public Release Considerations

**Your Questions Answered: Should this dashboard be released publicly? What might Quidax say?**

---

## Quick Answer

**Most of the dashboard is safe to release publicly without Quidax's explicit approval.**

But there are three sections where Quidax **might** have concerns. See below.

---

## What IS Safe to Release (No Consent Needed)

### 1. The Competitive Analysis

**What it is**: Comparing Quidax to Yellow Card, Busha, Luno, Roqqu.

**Why it's safe**: This is publicly available analysis.
- All comparison data came from public sources (websites, Twitter, news articles)
- You're not revealing Quidax's private information
- You're not claiming Quidax endorses this analysis
- Other crypto companies publish competitive analyses all the time

**Quidax's likely reaction**: "We don't control what analyses people publish about us."

**Examples**: Coinbase has published competitive analyses on their blog. This is normal.

---

### 2. The Live Price Ticker

**What it is**: Showing Quidax USDT/NGN and other prices in real-time.

**Why it's safe**: 
- Quidax's API is public
- Anyone can fetch these prices
- You're using their PUBLIC API (not secret endpoints)
- You're giving them credit (mentioning Quidax as the source)

**Quidax's likely reaction**: "We published a public API for this. Go ahead."

---

### 3. The B2B Opportunity Analysis

**What it is**: Estimating how big Quidax's potential B2B business could be.

**Why it's safe**:
- These are estimates, not facts about Quidax's operations
- The TAM (Total Addressable Market) numbers come from World Bank, not Quidax
- You're not claiming Quidax has published these revenues

**BUT — flag**: If you say "Quidax could make $X million", Quidax might worry you're over-promising to investors or underselling themselves.

**Recommended disclosure**: Add a sentence: "These are analyst estimates, not Quidax forecasts or targets."

## What MIGHT Need Permission (Check First)

### 1. Competitor Rankings

**The concern**: You've ranked Quidax against competitors on things like "SEC License Status", "B2B API Maturity", etc.

**Does it need permission?** PROBABLY NOT, but Quidax might worry:
- Are the rankings accurate? (If they say Quidax is #1, they won't mind. If they're #2, they might argue.)
- Am I underrating their compliance? ("You said we don't have EFCC SAR pipeline coverage — that's not true!")

**What to do**: Make rankings SMALL and include a confidence level. Don't use star ratings (⭐⭐⭐) — they feel like scorecards. Use descriptive text instead.

**Example of SAFE**:
> "Yellow Card has SEC-registered VASP status in three US states (confidence: High, verified via [SEC LINK]). Quidax operates under Nigerian AML/CFT frameworks (confidence: Medium, inferred from regulatory environment)."

**Example of RISKY**:
> "Quidax: 2/5 stars for compliance. Yellow Card: 4/5 stars."

---

### 2. Revenue Figures & Customer Counts

**What's in the dashboard**: "Busha: 1M+ users", "Yellow Card: $50M+ B2B volume"

**Does it need permission?** MAYBE.

**Why Quidax might care**:
- If you say "Quidax: unknown number of users" and competitors have published numbers, it looks like Quidax is hiding
- If you estimate Quidax's revenue and it's wrong, Quidax has to publicly deny it (annoying for them)

**What to do**: 
1. Only cite numbers Quidax has actually published
2. If you DON'T have a number for Quidax, just leave it blank. Don't estimate.
3. Always include confidence level and source for competitor numbers

**Example**:
- ❌ **Don't say**: "Quidax does ~$50M/year volume" (you're guessing)
- ✅ **Do say**: "Quidax does not publish volume figures (as of May 2026). Estimated range $20-100M based on [SOURCE]" (you're transparent)

---

### 3. The "Counter-Thesis" Section

**What it is**: "Here's how this analysis could be wrong."

**Does it need permission?** NO. But be fair.

**Things NOT to say**:
- ❌ "Quidax's API might be down all the time" (implies it's unreliable)
- ❌ "Quidax might be insolvent" (serious accusation)

**Things that ARE OK to say**:
- ✅ "If stablecoins lose regulatory status in Nigeria, the B2B opportunity shrinks"
- ✅ "If Quidax focuses on retail instead of B2B, this analysis is moot"
- ✅ "If a major competitor launches with 10% lower fees, Quidax's margin assumptions break"

These are macro risks, not accusations.

## What Needs Permission (Definitely)

### 1. If the dashboard is positioned as "Quidax's own pitch document"

If you present this to investors and say **"This is Quidax's view"**, you MUST have permission.

If you say **"This is my independent analysis of Quidax"**, you probably don't.

**Example**:
- ❌ **Don't do this**: "Quidax: B2B revenue forecast" (implies Quidax wrote it)
- ✅ **Do this**: "Analyst forecast for potential Quidax B2B revenue" (you wrote it)

---

### 2. If you're using Quidax's logo or brand heavily

**Does it need permission?** YES.

Every screenshot/mention of "Quidax" is using their trademark. Trademark law says you need permission for commercial use.

**What to do**:
- Add a footer: "Quidax is a trademark of Quidax Technologies Limited. This dashboard is independent and not endorsed by Quidax."

---

### 3. If the dashboard is being sold or monetized

**Does it need permission?** YES, definitely.

If this is a paid dashboard or you're charging to share it, you need to license it from Quidax or get explicit permission.

## Specific Questions You Asked

### "Would Quidax be okay with this being public?"

**Most likely YES, if you**:
1. Add a disclaimer: "This is independent analysis, not endorsed by or affiliated with Quidax."
2. Link to sources (especially Quidax's public API and website)
3. Don't make up Quidax revenue figures
4. Don't claim this is Quidax's official forecast

**Quidax might have concerns if you**:
1. Present this as Quidax's own internal document
2. Make wild revenue claims ("Quidax could make $2B/year")
3. Include inaccurate competitor rankings
4. Use their logo without attribution

### "Is competitor ranking data sensitive?"

**Not usually.** Everyone publishes competitor analyses.

But check:
- Is the rank FACTUALLY CORRECT? If you say "Roqqu is #1 in Nigeria" but they're actually #3, Roqqu will complain.
- Are you being FAIR? Don't rank Quidax on dimensions where no one has data.

**Example of UNFAIR**: "Luno: 5/5 for global reach. Quidax: 2/5" — unfair because Luno is a global company, Quidax is regional.

### "Should I ask Quidax before releasing?"

**Legally**: No, you don't have to.

**Practically**: YES, ask them. Here's why:
1. They might give you better data ("Our B2B volume is actually $X")
2. They might correct errors ("We DO have EFCC SAR coverage")
3. They might thank you and share the dashboard (free marketing for you)
4. They might ask you to remove one section (and you can negotiate)

**What to send them**:
> "Hi Buchi, I did an independent analysis of Quidax's B2B opportunity. It's factual and cites public sources. I'd like your permission to release it publicly. Would you like to review it first and suggest corrections?"
